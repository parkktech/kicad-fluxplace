### 9.2 Where the RF limits come from (read-only trace)

### 9.3 Ticket B14: length-budget feasibility and chain re-placement

Branch `w64-lenbudget`. Extends budget.py (net_bounds), placegaps.py (`net-bound` / `net-budget` kinds, `pull` rung), preroute.py:79-111
(`net_over_budget`, which already computes a fixed-parts floor and emits ANCHOR_SPACING), netclass.py (budget provenance). No new command or
flag; board-agnostic (chains found from the netlist: source pin, series two-pin parts, load pin).

Four quantities per budgeted path, written to the gap row and the review finding:

| Symbol | Meaning | How |
|---|---|---|
| T | budget in mm, with provenance: `required` (cited datasheet or reference design), `derived` (loss or edge-rate allocation with its inputs), `default` (tool class default) | netclass contract row gains `budget_src`; a `default` budget is printed as such in every finding |
| B_current | free-space lower bound at the current poses | exists (A8): octilinear, pad-access points, summed per mandatory copper leg |
| B_all | certified lower bound over all permitted poses | per leg, the minimum octilinear distance between the two terminals over each part's pose domain (fixed part = one pose; movable part = legal seats x rotations x sides from the legaliser's seat grid, keep-outs and side rules applied), with one pose per footprint shared by all its terminals. Exact enumeration over the seat grid for chains up to 4 movable parts; beyond that a relaxation that is a valid lower bound (each leg minimised independently, which can only under-estimate). A sampled best pose is never reported as B_all |
| U | shortest verified routed length found so far | from the routed board or a ghost lay of the chain at contract width and layers; absent until a route exists |

Decision logic (tolerance = one grid step):

| Test, in order | Verdict | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| B_all > T | `infeasible under declared fixed parts and pose domains` | owner question row naming the fixed parts and T's provenance; never a rung |
| U exists and U <= T | `feasible`; placement and route kept as the witness | none |
| B_current > T (and B_all <= T) | `current poses excluded` | placement: chain re-placement rung |
| otherwise (B_current <= T < U or no U) | `unresolved: routing detour or access` | route/close: re-lay of that net with the budget as a limit; if it fails twice, chain re-placement with a tighter target |

Repair. `pull` stays for the case where one movable end carries most of the excess. New rung `chain` in placegaps DISPATCH for
`net-bound` / `net-budget`: re-place source, series parts and load as one group, legal rotations and selected neighbour moves allowed,
every budget of the chain a hard constraint, the pose from the B_all enumeration as the seed, least displacement among feasible sets,
support distances not worse (the 15 -> 0 and 13 -> 1 gains are guarded by the existing never-worse rows). Crystals: both oscillator legs,
crystal orientation and both load capacitors in one group. Accepted only through the placement gate, then ghost-laid to obtain U.

Acceptance tests (golden, through the stage executor):
- `len_fixed_infeasible`: two fixed connectors 40 mm apart, T = 30: verdict infeasible, an owner row, no rung attempt, stage time under 5 s.
- `len_chain`: source, two series parts, load; `pull` on one part cannot meet T, the chain rung does; supports not worse.
- `len_detour`: B_current <= T, routed U > T around an obstacle: verdict unresolved, dispatched to route, not to placement.
- `len_ball_certified`: B_all equals the brute-force minimum on a 3-part chain; with the relaxation it is never above it.
- `len_default_src`: a class-default budget is printed as `default`, a `[placement.budget."NET"]` row with a reason as `required`/`derived`.
