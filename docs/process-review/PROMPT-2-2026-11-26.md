Four problems remain where we want a design opinion, not a patch. Please answer each with: the most likely root cause given the evidence, what to measure next to confirm it, and the change you would make. Then rank them by impact on reaching zero opens.

## 1. The repair ("close") stage barely runs
Close dispatches each open gap to a ladder of "rungs" (each a child process: rip/re-route a region, widen power, re-lay a pair group, fill copper islands, fix edge slivers, ...). A candidate is accepted only if it strictly improves a lexicographic score against the incumbent and adds no new error-severity DRC key.
Measured on the real board (40 min budget, refusal record on):
- 4 attempts in 40 min. 1 accepted (power widen: power-deficit magnitude 171.62 -> 169.37). 3 refused, none by the acceptance rules: pair-group re-lay exited 1 (crash), copper-island fill timed out after 434 s, edge-sliver wrote no board.
- An earlier board version: 32 attempts, 0 accepts.
- Per-rung timeouts as a fraction of the close budget are now added (copper-island 5 %).
Questions: Is a ladder of ~20 independent rung programs the right structure if individual rungs are this fragile? Should close instead be a small number of well-tested operations (your measure / diagnose / propose / execute / verify_and_commit) with rungs as parameterisations? How would you budget attempts across gaps so slow rungs cannot starve the rest? What would you log per attempt to make the next failure diagnosable without a rerun?

## 2. Placement: 12 pins with no escape
After the support-part placement fix (decoupling caps too far 15 -> 0, other support parts too far 13 -> 1), the floorplan gate still fails its breakout check: 12 pins have no legal escape (pin -> via site or channel). The same 12 were blocked before the fix. They are fine-pitch IC pins; an earlier analysis put ~13 of them on one part (U600) next to a thermal solder-mask opening on the other side, where vias are forbidden (settled design: the opening is stripped before routing and re-cut after). A forbidden-region model shared by placement, fanout, pairs and repair is being tested now.
Questions: Should a no-escape pin be a placement gap (move/rotate the part or its neighbours) or a routing gap (escape on another layer, via-in-pad, dog-bone direction)? How would you decide automatically which it is? Is it sound to treat the mask opening as a hard via keep-out during placement but strip it during routing, or does that make placement and routing disagree?

## 3. Joint pair-exit planning cannot be shown to fix the real failure
Real failure: Ethernet pair P1 is walled in at a 10-pin 0.5 mm-pitch protection part by pair P0's escape copper. Laying P1 first closed P1 but then PCIE_RX and a USB3 TX pair (ranked higher) failed; x10 and x30 router budgets changed nothing.
Built per your round-2 advice: per-component exit bundles (terminal variant, layer, heading, via site, neighbour-pin access, length margin), joint selection by backtracking with forward checking (10 000 expansion cap), ghost-lay of at most 5 shortlisted candidates with the production lay, verification set = targets plus every equal-or-higher-rank pair whose straight corridor crosses the part (by geometry), neighbourhood grows only to a part a failed probe names, chosen exits reserved hard until the owner pair is laid.
Problem: we could not build a synthetic microboard where the current flow fails and the planner fixes it. Sweeping the channel width, at 1.5 mm both pairs stay open with or without the plan; at 1.6 mm both lay with or without it. The W16 comparison (pairs stage with the planner on vs off) is running; results will be appended below.
Questions: What does this suggest the conflict model is missing (e.g. via-site competition, the far-end corridor, layer-change cost, the router's own ordering inside one call)? How would you construct a minimal reproducer from the real board instead of synthetically (crop region + fixed boundary terminals)? If the W16 run shows no difference, what is the next hypothesis?

## 4. Length budgets that repair cannot meet
8 nets are over their routed-length budget (examples: crystal 20 mm vs 12; RF 62 vs 35, 43 vs 26.5; SMA feed 70 vs 60). The placement gate now detects them (free-space octilinear bound between pad-access points, per segment through series parts) and dispatches them to a "pull" rung that moves movable parts toward their anchor; it has not fixed any of them yet. Some ends are fixed parts (connectors at the board edge, the module).
Questions: How should the tool separate "impossible with these fixed parts" (an owner/budget question) from "possible but the pull rung is weak"? Is moving one part toward an anchor the right repair, or should it be a small local re-placement of the whole chain (source, series parts, load) with the budget as a hard constraint? For RF, is a budget violation of 1.7x on a 50 ohm line a placement problem or a budget that should be re-derived from loss (dB) instead of length?
